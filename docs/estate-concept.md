# COFFWE — Estate Concept

Status: **finalized** (v1.0, locked 2026-11-10) — this is the ground truth the simulator, data model, and every AI brain are built against. Changing any of these facts later is a deliberate decision, not a drive-by edit.

COFFWE is a fully imaginary, 50-year-old shade coffee estate in the Western Ghats highlands, elevation 1200–1300 m. It grows Arabica and Robusta in equal numbers across 10 unequal blocks, intercropped with pepper and areca nut, surrounded by native forest shade trees, and run with an explicit organic/"go green" transition in mind.

The **estate core** (processing, storage, fertilizer lab + vermicompost + biogas, plant nursery, cow + chicken sheds, worker housing) sits geographically centered among the blocks — adjacent to B6 and B7 — rather than at the estate edge, for roughly equal access from every block.

| Crop / product | Role |

## Native shade & forest trees (ecological role, not harvested)

Silver Oak (naturalized, standard pepper-support shade tree) plus 20 native Western Ghats species: Dadap (Erythrina), Wild Jack (Ainili), Jackfruit, Indian Rosewood, Indian Beech (Honge), Kindal, Matti, Indian Laurel (Punna), Cluster Fig (Atthi), Banyan, Ironwood (Nagasampige), Hopea, White Dammar (Dhoopa), Wild Nutmeg, Wild Cinnamon, Kadamba, Jamun (Naval), Indian Kino (Honne), Mango, Bamboo.

## Livestock & the organic loop

Cows and chickens are support livestock, not primary profit centers — they close a waste-to-input loop: cow dung + processing wastewater feed a **biogas unit**; pulp waste + dung feed **vermicompost pits**; both feed the **fertilizer & soil lab**, which blends organic inputs and runs per-block soil tests. Chickens forage near block edges for natural insect control.

## Water & irrigation

Stream 1 runs past the Robusta blocks near the entrance; Stream 2 runs through the steep B5 valley. Both flow down from the ridge toward the 1200 m exit. A small check-dam/reservoir on Stream 1 feeds a drip/sprinkler network, used only as a dry-season backup, not routine irrigation.

## Processing & nursery

- **Wet processing:** cherry receiving/sorting → water-efficient pulper with recycling settling tanks → fermentation tanks → washing channel. Pulp waste routes to the vermicompost pits.
- **Dry processing:** raised sun-drying beds for clear days, plus a **parabolic polyhouse dryer** with retractable curtains that close automatically when the rain sensor trips — the first natural candidate for a real automated actuation once the digital twin's closed loop exists (Phase 8).
- **Nursery:** raises Arabica/Robusta saplings, pepper cuttings, areca saplings, and native shade-tree saplings for the estate's replanting cycle and corridor reforestation.

## Wildlife corridor & conflict prevention

The estate borders reserve forest at its upper edge (near B9/B10). Deterrence favors avoidance over confrontation:

- Forest buffer strip (B10's 3 ac) left uncultivated, connecting to the reserve forest.
- Beehive fencing along the corridor-facing boundary — doubles as the honey line.
- Motion/IR early-warning sensors, notifying workers to move rather than confront wildlife.
- Chili-rope/smoke deterrent as a traditional secondary barrier.
- Thorny native hedge on the crop-facing side of the buffer.
- Dusk/dawn quiet-hours protocol for workers near the corridor.

## Organic pest management (IPM)

Monitor → analyze → intervene organically, instead of routine spraying:

| Monitor | Signal | Organic intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Pheromone traps | Coffee berry borer catch counts | *Beauveria bassiana* biological spray, trap density increase |
| Sticky traps | Aphid/whitefly counts | Neem-based botanical spray; encourage ladybird predators |
| Leaf-wetness + humidity sensors | Coffee leaf rust risk window | Preventive copper (Bordeaux) spray only above risk threshold |
| Chickens | Ongoing ground-insect foraging | Passive, continuous near block edges |
| Nursery quarantine | New sapling inspection | Hold before planting out — biosecurity |

Trap counts + weather data are the feed the Pest Control AI brain's outbreak-probability model will train on in Phase 6.

## Tagging convention

Every tree, vine, and coffee plant gets a unique tag: `<Block>·<Species>·<Sequence>`, e.g. `B3·RB·00147` for Robusta tree #147 in Block 3. Species codes: `RB` Robusta, `AR` Arabica, `PP` Pepper, `AN` Areca nut, plus a short code per native species.
