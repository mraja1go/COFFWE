Livestock & the organic loop

Support livestock closes a waste→input loop rather than acting as a primary profit centre — this is the backbone of the "go green" transition the Sustainability brain will eventually track.

01

Cows

Housed in the estate's central core. Dung feeds the biogas unit and the vermicompost pits; surplus milk is for estate use.

02

Chickens

Forage near block edges for natural insect control; eggs for estate use, informal surplus sale.

03

Biogas unit

Converts dung + wet-processing wastewater into cooking/processing fuel.

04

Vermicompost pits

Converts pulp waste + dung into organic fertilizer, feeding the fertilizer lab.

05

Fertilizer & soil lab

Prepares compost/vermicompost blends and runs soil pH/NPK tests per block.

Water & irrigation

Two streams cross the estate — Stream 1 past the Robusta blocks near the entrance, Stream 2 through the steep valley at B5 — both flowing down from the ridge toward the exit at 1200 m. A small check-dam/reservoir on Stream 1 feeds a drip/sprinkler network used only in dry-season months when rainfall drops below irrigation threshold, kept as a dry-season backup rather than routine irrigation.

Processing & nursery

Processing, storage, the lab, nursery, livestock sheds, and worker housing all sit together in the estate's central core — the shortest average walk from any of the ten blocks.

Wet processing

Cherry receiving & sorting → water-efficient pulper with recycling settling tanks → fermentation tanks → washing channel. Pulp waste routes to the vermicompost pits; wash water routes through settling before reuse or release.

Dry processing

Raised sun-drying beds for clear days, plus a parabolic polyhouse dryer with retractable side curtains — closed automatically when the rain sensor trips, so drying isn't lost to Western Ghats monsoon showers. This is the first candidate for a real automated actuation in the digital twin's closed loop.

Plant nursery

Raises Arabica/Robusta saplings, pepper cuttings, areca saplings, and native shade-tree saplings — feeding both the estate's 50-year replanting cycle and reforestation of the forest buffer/corridor.

Wildlife corridor & conflict prevention

The estate borders reserve forest at its upper edge (near B9/B10) — real Western Ghats estates deal with elephant, wild boar, and macaque movement through exactly this kind of edge. The approach favours deterrence and early warning over confrontation:

  • Forest buffer strip (B10's 3 ac) left uncultivated as an undisturbed passage connecting to the reserve forest, so animals don't need to cross cropped blocks.
  • Beehive fencing along the corridor-facing boundary — elephants reliably avoid areas with active bee colonies, and it doubles as the honey line.
  • Motion/IR early-warning sensors on the corridor edge, notifying workers to move rather than confront.
  • Chili-rope / smoke deterrent as a traditional, low-tech secondary barrier.
  • Thorny native hedge on the crop-facing side of the buffer as a soft physical barrier.
  • Dusk/dawn quiet-hours protocol for workers near the corridor during known movement windows.

Tagging convention

Every tree, vine, and coffee plant gets a unique tag for individual tracking: <Block>·<Species>·<Sequence>, e.g. B3·RB·00147 for Robusta tree #147 in Block 3. Species codes: RB Robusta, AR Arabica, PP Pepper, AN Areca nut, plus a short code per native species. At ≈44,950 tagged individuals, this is a deliberately data-engineering-scale problem — big enough to force real schema and pipeline decisions, small enough to run comfortably on the free-tier cloud resources from the roadmap.
